# ESM-2 Mechanism Project — Synthesis

**Date:** 2026-05-28

**The question.** Can a frozen protein language model (ESM-2 650M) tell you not just *whether* a mutation is harmful, but *how* it's harmful — gain-of-function, loss-of-function, or dominant-negative (the "mechanism")? That mechanism call is what actually changes clinical decisions, and it's where existing tools are weakest. The project ran ~25 experiments to answer it, and the answer is a clean, well-defended **no — and here's exactly why.**

**Thread 1: Is ESM-2 bad at mechanism? (Yes, and it's not even close.)** Early results showed a linear probe on mutation embeddings scoring at chance. The natural rebuttal — "maybe the signal is there but nonlinear" — was tested and failed: MLPs, kNN, trees all gave the same null under honest evaluation. The culprit is **family leakage**. Mutation classifiers look decent only because they recognize which protein family a gene belongs to (genes in a family share a mechanism ~75% of the time), not because they read anything about the mutation. Hold out whole families and the signal collapses. Crucially, nonlinearity *rescues stability prediction but not mechanism* (result_21, result_23) — proving mechanism signal isn't merely hidden, it's genuinely absent in any family-transferable form.

**Thread 2: What works instead? (Gene biology, not the embedding.)** Once mechanism-from-sequence was ruled out, the project showed that **proteome/gene-level features** — gnomAD constraint, ClinGen dosage sensitivity, paralog count, structural priors from Badonyi & Marsh — beat the full 1280-dimensional ESM-2 embedding (best F1 ≈ 0.51 vs 0.38), and that adding ESM-2 on top contributes nothing. The mechanism information lives at the level of "what kind of gene is this," which a language model reading one sequence at a time can't see.

**Thread 3: Is the pipeline trustworthy? (Yes — verified with positive controls.)** To prove the null wasn't a broken pipeline, the same machinery was pointed at tasks where signal *should* exist: pathogenicity (AUROC ~0.89), protein stability (nonlinearly recoverable, ~0.75), and enzyme-type classification (family-split F1 0.655). All worked. The pipeline detects signal when it's there; mechanism just isn't.

**The unifying result (result_23).** Inside one frozen model, transferability is **task- and probe-dependent**: pathogenicity is a linear, family-universal axis — and that axis turns out to *be conservation* (ESM-2's own masked-LM likelihood alone beats the embedding, so this is characterization, not a new representation); stability is a nonlinear cross-family manifold; mechanism transfers at none. A single model holds all three regimes at once.

**What's genuinely novel vs. confirmatory.** Honestly assessed: most embedding findings are careful **characterization** of things the field broadly knew (direction-over-magnitude, pathogenicity ≈ conservation, AlphaMissense's in- vs out-of-distribution behavior). The standout original idea is the **leakage fraction** — a seed-invariant, train-free diagnostic that converts "k-fold can leak" into "leakage on this dataset is 62.8%, and here's how much your headline number shrinks." That's the most publishable concept, but still one analytic derivation and one cross-dataset replication short of being a defensible claim rather than a promising note.

**Bottom line.** This is a rigorous **negative-result + characterization study**: ESM-2 cannot predict variant mechanism, the apparent ability others report is largely a data-splitting artifact, gene-level biology does the job the embedding can't, and the project's lasting methodological contribution — if the derivation lands — is a way to *measure* that artifact before anyone trains a model.
